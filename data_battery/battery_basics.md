# Understanding battery health
Battery charge percentage describes how much stored energy is available now. Battery health describes performance and remaining capacity compared with the battery when new. A full charge does not mean full original capacity.

Gradual loss of runtime can indicate normal aging. Sudden shutdowns, charging errors or poor starting can have multiple causes, including battery aging, charger problems, connections, temperature or device faults. Symptoms alone cannot reliably diagnose a battery or prove it is safe. A qualified technician can test the battery and charging system.

Check the device's built-in health report if available and follow its service recommendations. There is no universal replacement percentage, lifespan in years or cycle count for all batteries. Give the device model, battery chemistry, age and symptoms for useful next steps. Electric vehicles, phones, laptops, power banks, car starting batteries and stationary backup systems need different guidance.

Prevent failure by following the product manual, using compatible charging equipment, avoiding physical damage and excessive heat, and responding promptly to warnings. Maintenance reduces avoidable risks but cannot guarantee that a battery will never fail. Questions about an exact model's voltage, jump starting, EV high-voltage repairs or internal pack repair require its official manual and qualified service.
